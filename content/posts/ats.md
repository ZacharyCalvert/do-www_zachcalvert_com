## Passing Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)

I'm gearing up to search for a position I want in supposedly one of the worst job markets since 2008.  As an engineer, I outlined the goals I had in preparing my next resume.  My identified objectives include:
- Get me through the ATS filters.
- Present work experience that I'm both proud of and would want to do again.
- Show my strengths and willingness to grow in technologies and roles as demand requires it.
- Be truthful in my work history and experience so that when prompted, I can speak clearly and without hesitation as to both the work and the impact.

I decided to focus on my work with engineering leadership, AI, and Cybersecurity.  My title at McKesson was _Principal Information Security Analyst_ and included a mix of DevOps, Cybersecurity responsibilities, AI integrations including Claude Security and VulnHunter, technical leadership including decision making and tool selection, IT administrator for GitHub and JFrog, Systems Architect, Software Engineer, and Azure Cloud Engineer.

Leaning on my McKesson experience, I built a list of projects I led and contributed to, as well as the impact they carried.  I handed both the project list and my resume to [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/) for iterating and improving my resume.  

With [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com/), I reduced my resume from 8 pages to 4, highlighted my applicable work experience, retained my work history for the ATS filters, and updated the work experience to properly read left to right while reducing the noise ATS filters would discard by using bulleted lists.

I was not very familiar with ATS as the last time I was in the job market, my skills were both in high demand and ATS hits were primarily focused on keywords.  Now with AI, ATS are looking for contextual clues, not just keywords.  After reading through several articles and guides on ATS passing, including articles from [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/how-to-beat-applicant-tracking-system), [CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/284414/applicant-tracking-system.html), and [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/news/story/resume-tips-to-beat-the-bots-6668940/), it looks like Gemini's feedback was on point.  

I plan to have 3 versions of my Resume, with the first one being taken for a spin as my [Principal Security Analyst resume](/zachcalvert_cs.pdf).
