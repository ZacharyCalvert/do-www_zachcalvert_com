## Impact at McKesson

I recently had an interview where we went very deep into my work history at McKesson.  I was happy to see that my interviewer was making a serious effort to understand both my impact at McKesson as well as some discrepancies regarding my titles.  I had three distinct job titles at McKesson:

1. Principal DevSecOps Engineer
2. Principal Solutions Architect
3. Principal Information Security Analyst

When I joined McKesson in 2022, I was hired on as an individual contributor for the Principal DevSecOps Engineer role.  At that time, I was under the impression I was joining a team.  In fact, I was hired to build a business case, plan a budget, and pitch the projects identified to the business.  My hiring manager acknowledged that this was more of a director role, but I'm not one to shy away from any task needed for success, so I set out to execute.  I met with engineering leads, individual contributors, directors, and multiple VPs to identify development friction and areas of automation I could fix.  

I identified missing components including certificate automation, secret management automation, a need for Terraform Enterprise to be onboarded, and a desire for Launch Darkly for feature flag management.  After taking the identified projects to the procurement and budgeting teams, I was given a deadline by the SVP and CTO to rework the projects into _one_ large project with *one large budget*.  I suspected successful budget discussions would require the business to choose selectively which efforts to fund and which not to, but I was learning how things worked and more than willing to rework the project materials.

A short while later, I was handing over materials outlining _one large project with one large budget_.  Unfortunately, the business decided the funding priority would go to other areas of the business, unrelated to automation or development operations.  This meant not only did I fail to secure funding to build a team, but I had no budget to execute on the tools and technologies the engineering leads expressed a desire for.  My hiring manager informed me this was common, and I would need to find an internal team to transfer to or risk severance.  All of this happening within my first three months at McKesson.  

Fortunately, I had made friends along the way with my engineering discussions.  Leads and VPs of the North American Pharmaceutical Distribution business unit offered me a position as Principal Solutions Architect, overseeing engineering for a 90B USD drug distribution supply chain.  I was happy to accept and for the next two years provided engineering services and decision making for the re-platforming of an SAP Hybris solution hosted on McKesson owned datacenters to an Azure Cloud native environment, focusing on Kubernetes hosted 12-factor applications.  This was an exciting effort, leading 450 engineers including full-time staff and contractors from around the world.  We had engineering presence in Europe, North America, South America, and Asia, spanning 4 continents.  

Sadly, we experienced a significant change in course two years later, where my boss, his counterpart, our CIO, and others departed.  Leadership was gutted and the contractors, set on timelines committed in their Statements-of-Work, were adamant the pace must be maintained.  My role became holding on for dear life performing 30+ code reviews of tens of thousands of lines of code per day on application changes I could not keep up with.  I was no longer leading and no longer really contributing.  My role went from solutions architect to rubber-stamping "approved" and a dangerous role for my career.

During this time, our CISO was also replaced and a new set of leaders came in to execute a Cybersecurity vision for McKesson.  A Principal Information Security Analyst position opened up, which I applied for and accepted.  I was back in my original DevSecOps seat, focusing on SAST, DAST, secret management, external attack surface area management, compliance, and audit efforts.  For the next two years I was leading a team building out custom GitHub Actions, scraping data from JFrog, GitHub, and more for tracking trends including MTTR and MTTD.  We were incorporating AI using MCP and Azure Databricks Genie for faster, more natural interactions with compliance and audit teams.  Looking back at what we accomplished in two years, I'm incredibly proud and incredibly grateful the right position opened up at the right time.  

Hopefully this clarifies my role history as well as my responsibilities.
